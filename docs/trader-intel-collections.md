# Trader Intel collections (v1)

Three anonymous, read-only **trader** rankings, separate from Discovery market cards and published Intel posts:

| GET                                | What qualifies                                                                                                                                                  |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `/intel/traders/big-moves`         | A verified purchase by a profitable trader, meaningful against their previous positions in the category, with a remaining increase and a recent comparable ask. |
| `/intel/traders/top-earners-today` | Positive realized category P&L over the trailing 24 hours, including losses and partial sales. Open appreciation is not income.                                 |
| `/intel/traders/winning-streaks`   | At least five consecutive qualifying profitable completed positions in the same category.                                                                       |

Supply `?category=sports` for one group; omit it for all segments. Sports, crypto and politics are always present in an unfiltered response. Other segments follow the observed Hunch catalog. No cursor: at most three distinct owner families per group. This is **not a leaderboard of every Polymarket user**. The producer examines a bounded shortlist: up to 50 active system-tracked Polymarket wallets among the 500 most recently active selector rows in seven days, prefiltered on existing positive 30-day metrics, a sample of 20 resolved observations, and the existing market-maker heuristic. Those metrics are for discovery only, not the published profit.

Read `windowEnd`, `freshness` and each group's `coverage`: `selectedWallets` is the shortlist, `observedWallets` the accounts evaluated this generation, `eligibleWallets` the distinct qualifying families for this group before top-three. Rankings are among the evaluated accounts. Fewer than three or zero results is normal; never fill from a different category. `insufficient_evidence` is distinct from `unsupported`. A missing/expired snapshot or unavailable cache returns **503** with `Retry-After`, not a fabricated empty ranking. A conditional GET can return **304**.

Each result has a public trader identifier, optional public name, category-specific achievement, observed history, realized performance, evidence timestamp, flags and a separately selected `latestOpenPosition` or `null`. Decimal amounts and ratios are strings in USDC/shares, prices are dollars per share. `relativeToTypical="2"` means twice the trader's median historical completed stake in the category. `sharesAfter` is immediately after the purchase, not today's balance. `latestOpenPosition.shares` is the reconciled holding; `entryPrice` here is the most recent qualifying increase's fill VWAP, **not** the cost basis of the entire holding. `completedPositions` is a lower bound: the greater of reconstructed economic closes and distinct supported venue-confirmed closed positions, not a lifetime provider counter. A missing, partial wallet-wide total stays null. Top-of-book is indicative for one share, not an executable quote for the entire amount; depth and 30-day ROI are null when not established.

### Progress and exclusion reasons

Partial publications do not end the sweep. They atomically publish all three tabs and checkpoint the frozen shortlist, its time window, remaining queue, completed candidates and reasons. Later snapshots can share a `windowEnd` while coverage increases. Check `snapshotId` or ETag for a new publication. Wallet selection excludes known protocol contracts before the final limit of 50.

Within a frozen window, validated provider pages are privately journaled by wallet, venue, endpoint, exact parameters and cursor. A successor process replays economic fields locally and asks the provider only for missing pages. Opaque cursors and wallet addresses are never exposed in public diagnostics. Wallet-wide pagination and the 90-request visit budget yield to other wallets instead of accepting a prefix or permanently dropping the wallet. A rejected cursor resets just its stream. Per-slice provider admission, per-condition backfill bounds and the 30-minute public window are unchanged. Recovery never relabels old positions as a new window.

The journal is lease-fenced, expires with the window and is unlinked when the corresponding terminal wallet decision is durably saved. It is limited to 384 pages, 4 MiB encoded and 24 MiB uncompressed per wallet/window; `wallet_page_capacity` means full evidence did not fit, not a loss or zero position. A real SQL statement timeout resolving token bindings is `market_lookup_timeout`, distinct from a provider error. Progress on a previously discarded roster cannot be reconstructed retroactively from aggregate public diagnostics.

Polymarket's status cursors do not pin a database snapshot. Position walks therefore use last-event-time order (not a price-dependent order) and check activity from the cutoff through completion of the three position walks. A position that moved between status lists and is absent from the assembled inventory leaves an economic evidence gap; those later trades are not counted as earnings or losses in the earlier window.

New snapshots include `diagnostics` for **this tab across the entire shortlist**, even with a category filter. Older cache entries may omit it. For example, `selectedWallets:50, attemptedWallets:18, pendingWallets:34, observedWallets:14, qualifiedWallets:3` is consistent with two terminal source failures and eleven exclusions. Pending includes both untouched wallets and scheduled retries. Counts satisfy:

- selected = observed + pending + wallets in `sourceFailures`;
- observed = qualified + wallets in `exclusions`.

Each observed wallet with no candidate for the tab contributes **one** reason: the furthest qualification check reached across its categories. For example, `typical_stake_unknown` is different from `below_typical_stake`; `daily_profit_unknown` is different from `daily_profit_not_positive`; `streak_boundary_unknown` is not a loss. Source failures such as `incomplete_pages` never count as filters or as a financial result. Counts are at publication, before family deduplication, category filtering and the three-card cap. Expired Big Moves are removed before publication; the HTTP read may also hide a quote that has expired since publication.

Normal completion reaches every wallet, not necessarily 50 successful observations: page limits, timeouts and identity errors are reported honestly. Venue cooldown preserves unfinished work and does not replace a good snapshot. If the frozen window has become unservable (30 minutes), a fresh selection prioritizes still-eligible unfinished wallets and does not carry old candidate evidence into a new window. A normally finished queue is deleted; abandoned private progress expires after the seven-day selection horizon.

## Evidence and ranking

A native CLOSED position can attest a fee-bearing exit only when its lifetime contains exactly one matched buy and sell, the shares and clocks reconcile, and the disclosed buy fee explains the difference between gross proceeds and native profit. The same test applies to losses. Partial sales, unknown fees, conflicting rows, or reopens do not get this attribution.

`coverage.resultScope=servable_hunch_catalog_positions` is the universe for the reported numbers. An unmapped contract is not a zero and can make the wallet-wide observed total unavailable. The venue's positions projection can omit inactive markets even with archived markets enabled; the API is not an account statement. Open and redeemable-loss walks include archived markets and use a zero dust floor. A historical immutable instrument may support a past result after migration, but cannot supply a live price unless the current contract and outcome still match. Related controller/proxy wallets share one ranking slot.

- Activity (all participant roles), open positions including archived, closed positions, redeemable losers and condition-scoped final payouts come from public Polymarket Data API v2. Do not add trades a second time. Cursor walks preserve row multiplicity, including identical fills in one transaction. Incoming transfers, conversions and other operations not safely costed cannot masquerade as directional buys. An out-of-catalog asset cannot supply a ranking fact.
- Match token, condition and outcome index to a single frozen ledger generation and the Hunch catalog. A live opportunity additionally requires a current canonical token, an active market accepting orders and a fresh ask. No background price-refresh requests, RPC, AI, execution, or account sessions are involved.
- Match reconstructed inventory and realized economics to the position observation (four-place source rounding). Weighted-average cost is allocated on partial sales. One complete exit is one close; reopening starts a new episode. Apply final binary payout to remaining shares at resolution, not claim time. Never add a later redeem twice. Unconfirmed, void and fractional results are not binary wins.
- If full replay fails, a native cumulative position P&L may be used for a window only when its boundaries are supported. For example, the entire position life inside 30 days proves the 30-day value, not which part was earned today. Unknown daily closes or results suppress that ranking rather than ignoring a possible loss. A known earlier gap does not erase an independently bounded later streak.
- Initial ranking policy: 14 days of trading history, 20 evidenced completed positions; positive category realized P&L and review of open losses. Ignore sub-$10 stakes, break-even and entries **above** 90¢ only in the streak calculation. Mixed wins/losses tied at the boundary do not establish an exact sequence. For moves, require a $500 purchase, at least one typical historical stake, a remaining increase, age under one day and an ask within five cents **and** 10% of fill VWAP. Ranking ties are deterministic. These are methodology defaults, not trading restrictions.
- Big Moves evaluates each retained qualifying increase; a later small top-up does not erase an earlier significant buy. A buy subsequently unwound is not made current by a later repurchase. The separate latest open trade can therefore differ from the highlighted move. `quote.validUntil` expires no later than the earlier of the quote TTL and known market close/expiration.
- Paired purchases of complementary outcomes that don't materially increase directional exposure are not Big Moves. The common 30-day stake comparison includes supported closed-position purchase amounts even when per-sale fees cannot be apportioned; an unknown relevant stake is not silently dropped to inflate the ratio. A claim, a later top-up, or an older selectively backfilled close cannot manufacture a streak. When a venue clock and the last known trade disagree, continuity is not assumed.
- Daily net earnings and close details are different evidence. An exact native realized total can qualify even if the individual profits cannot be allocated: in that case `closeDetailsComplete=false`, `completedPositionCount24h=null` and `largestProfitableClose=null`. Unknown daily profits **or losses** cannot qualify. The economic `windowEnd` is frozen; `quote.asOf` can be later when a current quote is observed in a subsequent collection slice.

## Operational boundaries

HTTP handlers only GET a bounded prepared Redis result. No SQL, upstream fetch, lease or refresh is triggered by a client request. One background builder holds a renewable lease; only the owner may atomically publish all three views. All new PostgreSQL data queries use `BEGIN READ ONLY`, statement and lock timeouts. Instrument hydration is deduplicated in batches of at most 250. No migration, index, cron, AI job or trading permission is required. Redis writes concern derived snapshots and coordination only; no orders/holdings/financial records are modified.

The background lane uses the shared provider limiter, two concurrent accounts and bounded page/request budgets. Cold activity windows advance eight pages per pass; the cursor stays tied to the original filters/end, then a bounded overlap is replaced to catch recent corrections without dropping equal fills. In-memory checkpoints are an optimization: after process restart the affected source window is reread. A high-volume wallet can also use a separately complete one-day activity window plus complete position pages and bounded condition history; such rows carry `position_window_reconciled`. A short window is never silently treated as 30 days of trading. The frozen cohort and completed rankings checkpoint separately under the Redis lease. Daily cold windows recheck corrections. Capacity and identity failures are coverage gaps, not completed holdings or sales. Per-wallet source and carry-in caps intentionally exclude some very high-volume portfolios. A source cooldown retains the last published version. Quotes older than five minutes no longer appear as current opportunities even on a cached snapshot; the entire version expires after 30 minutes. No new indices are required.

On develop: `pnpm -F api test:trader-collections` or `bun apps/api/src/trader-collections-tests.ts` from the workspace root. The fixtures exercise all three real HTTP serializers and OpenAPI paths without a provider or server startup. Do not start the production API/worker merely to validate a patch; a real-data preview can inject a read-only repository and public GET transport instead.

## Verification on 12 November 2026

The final producer SELECTs ran under bounded read-only PostgreSQL 16 transactions (5s statement timeout). For a shortlist of 50, the first generic plan took 410 ms (967 shared hits / 1,436 reads) and the subsequent warm custom plan 9 ms (2,390 hits / 0 reads). For 250 mixed frozen bindings those figures were 1,004 ms (3,516 hits / 1,947 reads) and 15 ms (5,429 hits / 0 reads). Empty cohort and instrument plans both took under 1 ms. None of the six production plans used a sequential table scan. A separate fixture-selection diagnostic over recent quotes timed out in 5s; that query is not part of the implementation. Cache state differed: these are observed costs, not a generic/custom comparison or an HTTP latency target. No database or venue calls are on the HTTP path.

The venue can report a redemption a few seconds before its separate resolutions feed timestamps the result. Require the same instrument, exact amount, confirmed payout and reconciled position; book the result once at the official resolution timestamp, never ahead of the window. For outstanding shares with a disclosed native cost/fee breakdown, reconcile the remaining basis and include entry fees once. A limited live sample tests evidence handling, not the number of qualifying cards that a full cohort or a future ranking window will produce.

The subsequent market-lookup revision avoided unnecessary event, venue and quote reads and repeated protocol-JSON extraction. With warmed generic plans on production PG16, 250 historical bindings took 13.5 → 6.6 ms (4,992 → 2,500 shared-buffer hits), 250 active ones 36.9 → 20.6 ms (11,231 → 6,495), and a mixed batch 27.1 → 14.8 ms (8,103 → 4,530). Empty and missing bindings stayed empty. Symmetric comparisons of the effective repository outputs matched across custom and generic plans. Warm timings are not a worst-case latency guarantee; the original timed-out request did not retain its exact bind values.
